Privacy & data

Privacy policy for the MACTORY FATCH prototype.

This notice describes the prototype review workflow, where submissions are stored, and the limits that remain before a public production launch.

Last updated: 26 October 2026 · Prototype policy, not legal advice

Important limitation. The current form collects text fields only; it does not upload identity documents or certificates. Do not send identity documents, bank details, trade secrets or confidential drawings through this prototype. Admin access uses a server-side secret exchanged for a short-lived HttpOnly session cookie, but this is still a small-team control rather than individual named accounts with MFA. The legal operator and monitored privacy contact must be added before public launch.

1. Who operates this prototype?

This is a product concept and local demonstration of a manufacturing-sourcing interface named MACTORY FATCH. A production operator's full legal name, postal address, privacy contact and jurisdiction-specific details must be added before public launch. No address, company registration or contact details are invented here.

2. Information the prototype may ask you to enter

Depending on the page, the interface can ask for a manufacturing brief, product and material requirements, quantity, budget, delivery target, notes, saved factories, RFQ status, or proposed factory-owner details such as legal name, entity type, registration identifiers, business contact details, city/state, processes and certifications. These are fields the interface offers; the demo does not require you to supply real values for a fictional walkthrough.

Do not enter identity documents, bank details, passwords, trade secrets, sensitive drawings, personal data about other people, or registration identifiers belonging to real businesses unless and until a properly secured production service and reviewed privacy notice are in place.

3. Where information is stored

Older RFQ drafts, saved profile IDs and some workspace preferences remain in the browser's localStorage; those are browser-local convenience data, not a secure cloud workspace. Submitted RFQs and supplier responses are stored in the private Supabase RFQ table when production is configured. Factory registration text is stored in the local data/registrations.json file during local development, or in the private Supabase table when the production environment is configured. The local JSON file is not encrypted at rest, so local development data must be fictional or non-sensitive and the local server must not be exposed publicly. In production, the Supabase service-role key must exist only in server-side environment variables; row-level security is enabled with no public policies. No file-upload control exists in this build, so documents are not collected by the form.

4. Optional AI processing and web research

The buyer page may provide optional AI-assisted brief extraction and supplier research when the Node.js server is running and an API key is configured by the operator. The user must opt in before sending a brief to the AI function. If enabled, the text of the brief is sent from the server to the configured AI provider. Supplier research may use a web-search tool and return links to third-party sources. These API requests may incur usage charges for the operator.

Do not put confidential or personal data into an AI prompt. AI may misunderstand requirements, omit facts, return stale or incomplete information, or misattribute claims. Search results and cited sources are candidate leads only; neither the presence of a source link nor an AI-generated summary verifies the supplier, legal registration, ownership, factory, capacity, certification or offer. Check relevant primary sources and inspect the supplier independently.

When AI is not configured, the prototype uses local parsing and heuristic ranking. That local mode does not send the brief to an AI service. The local Node.js server also applies a basic in-memory rate limit to AI endpoints; the demo is not designed as a production security control.

5. Sharing and third-party sites

Registration submissions are sent to the configured application server and are visible to authorized reviewers through the private review endpoint. They are not automatically sent to factories or publicly listed. The current registration screening uses deterministic rules and does not send registration information to an AI provider. If you separately choose an AI feature on the buyer page, the selected sourcing brief is sent to the configured AI provider as described above. The site also links to external official lookup portals, such as GST, MCA and Udyam, which are operated by their respective organizations and have their own privacy and usage terms. MACTORY FATCH does not control those websites.

6. Retention and deletion

Verification is designed to be purpose-limited. Pending and “needs more information” applications are eligible for automatic deletion 90 days after initial submission. The cleanup runs when registration/admin workflows are used and is also configured as a daily Vercel scheduled task when the deployment supports scheduled functions and CRON_SECRET is configured. Rejected submissions have their submitted payload, raw registration number, legal name and reviewer notes scrubbed immediately; the minimal status record is eligible for deletion after 30 days. When a decision is recorded, raw registration numbers, addresses, contact names, phone, notes and other non-public fields are removed immediately. Business email is also removed unless the factory has both consented to public listing and separately opted in to RFQ notifications; in that case it is retained in a separate private routing field and never shown publicly. An approved listing retains only the fields needed for its public profile and a keyed HMAC fingerprint of the registration identifier to reduce duplicate applications; the fingerprint is not the raw registration number and the HMAC key must remain secret. If the owner did not consent to public listing, the public-profile payload is cleared. Public listing fields remain until the listing is removed or corrected. Cleanup is not a guarantee that backups or infrastructure logs are instantly erased; those systems need their own documented retention settings. Requests sent to an external AI provider or third-party website are subject to that provider's own terms.

7. Security

The administrator credential is kept in server-side environment variables and is not embedded in public HTML/JavaScript or saved to browser storage. Successful sign-in creates a 30-minute, HttpOnly, Secure-on-HTTPS, SameSite=Strict cookie; sign-out clears it. This reduces exposure but is not a substitute for named administrator accounts, MFA, durable rate limiting, monitoring and incident response. The local JSON store is not encrypted at rest. The current form has no document upload; reviewers should inspect public official sources and record a minimal reference, not collect copies of identity documents in this application. Before accepting sensitive evidence, implement a dedicated private storage workflow with strict access, encryption, deletion and audit controls. Never expose the local development server to the public internet.

8. Your choices and rights

Public listing consent is optional and separate from the consent needed to process an application. A factory can request private verification without agreeing to public display. You can request correction, withdrawal of public listing consent or deletion through the operator once a monitored privacy contact is published. The current build still requires the operator to handle individual requests; automated retention cleanup is not a self-service deletion portal. RFQs are retained to operate buyer-supplier conversations and are scheduled for deletion after 365 days by default (configurable between 90 and 730 days). Cleanup execution and backup deletion still need production monitoring and documented procedures. The production service must publish the legal operator, purposes, lawful basis where applicable, contact details and response process for relevant jurisdictions.

9. Changes to this notice

This notice may be revised as the product changes. The production policy must describe actual deployed behavior, vendors, legal bases where applicable, purposes, retention periods, transfer arrangements, contact details and effective date—not merely planned features.

10. Contact

No production privacy contact has been supplied for this prototype, so none is invented here. Before public launch, the operator must add a monitored privacy/contact channel and legal identity. For now, treat this as development documentation, not a formal channel for regulatory or data-subject requests.

RFQ delivery

When a factory explicitly opts in to buyer RFQ notifications, its business email is retained in a separate private field solely to route enquiries and notify the factory. That email is not published in the public directory. Buyer contact details and submitted specifications are shared with the selected factory for the specific enquiry.

RFQs and private tracking links are stored to operate the enquiry workflow. Anyone holding a buyer tracking link may view that enquiry, so keep it confidential.
